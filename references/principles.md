# Paul Graham Principles Database

Core principles, frameworks, and quotes extracted from 50 essays.

## §1. Idea Validation — "Don't try to think of startup ideas. Look for problems."

### Core Principles

1. **Well vs Broad**: Many people wanting something a little (broad) vs a few wanting it desperately (well). Always choose the well.
   - "When you have an idea for a startup, ask yourself: who wants this right now? Who wants this so much that they'll use it even when it's a crappy version one made by a two-person startup they've never heard of?" — [How to Get Startup Ideas](http://www.paulgraham.com/startupideas.html)

2. **Sitcom Idea Detection**: "Yeah, I could see using that" = danger sign. Only "I need this right now" counts.
   - The pet social network example: sounds plausible, but nobody desperately wants it.

3. **Organic vs Manufactured**: The best ideas grow naturally from the founder's life.
   - "Live in the future, then build what's missing." — [Organic Startup Ideas](http://www.paulgraham.com/organic.html)
   - Apple, Yahoo, Google, Facebook all started as side projects, not companies.

4. **Path Out**: Start in a narrow market, but there must be a path to a bigger one.
   - Facebook: Harvard → all colleges → everyone
   - Microsoft: Altair Basic → other machines → OS → applications

5. **Schlep Blindness**: Unconsciously avoiding tedious work makes you miss great ideas.
   - Stripe: Thousands of programmers knew payment processing was painful. Nobody wanted to deal with it.
   - "The most dangerous thing about our dislike of schleps is that much of it is unconscious." — [Schlep Blindness](http://www.paulgraham.com/schlep.html)

6. **Unsexy Filter**: Don't avoid problems just because they seem boring.
   - "Make something unsexy that people will pay you for." — [How to Get Startup Ideas](http://www.paulgraham.com/startupideas.html)

7. **Frighteningly Ambitious Ideas**: The biggest ideas get unconsciously filtered out because they threaten your identity.
   - "The biggest ideas seem to threaten your identity: you wonder if you'd have enough ambition to carry them through." — [Frighteningly Ambitious Startup Ideas](http://www.paulgraham.com/ambitious.html)

### Self-Diagnosis Questions
- Would I use this if someone else built it?
- Who specifically needs this desperately?
- How are they solving this problem right now?
- Can I see a path from this niche to a larger market?

## §2. Early Growth — "Startups take off because the founders make them take off."

### Core Principles

1. **Do Things That Don't Scale**: Recruit users manually in the early days.
   - Stripe: Mid-demo, "Can I set it up for you right now?" (The Collison installation)
   - Airbnb: Personally visited NYC hosts to take professional photos
   - "Recruit users manually. Nearly all startups have to." — [Do Things that Don't Scale](http://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html)

2. **Delight → Growth**: Make a few people ecstatic and they'll spread the word.
   - "It's better to make a few people really happy than to make a lot of people semi-happy." — [Startups in 13 Sentences](http://www.paulgraham.com/13sentences.html)

3. **Growth = Compass**: Use growth rate as your compass. It's the basis for every decision.
   - 5-7% weekly growth = good startup
   - 10% weekly growth = 142x per year
   - "If you get growth, everything else tends to fall into place." — [Startup = Growth](http://www.paulgraham.com/growth.html)

4. **Ramen Profitable**: Earn just enough for the founders to live on ramen — that's freedom.
   - Independence from investors
   - "The most important thing ramen profitability buys you is time." — [Ramen Profitable](http://www.paulgraham.com/ramenprofitable.html)

5. **Startup = Growth**: The very definition of a startup is a company designed to grow fast.

### The 13 Sentences Checklist
1. Pick good cofounders
2. Launch fast
3. Let your idea evolve
4. Understand your users
5. Better to make a few users love you than many sort-of like you
6. Offer surprisingly good customer service
7. Measure what you do
8. Spend little
9. Get ramen profitable
10. Avoid distractions
11. Don't get demoralized
12. Don't give up
13. Deals fall through

Source: [Startups in 13 Sentences](http://www.paulgraham.com/13sentences.html)

## §3. Survival & Judgment — "Default Alive or Default Dead?"

### Core Principles

1. **Default Alive/Dead Calculation**: At your current revenue and expense trajectory, will you reach profitability before running out of money?
   - Being default dead without knowing it is the most dangerous state.
   - Source: [Default Alive or Default Dead?](http://www.paulgraham.com/aord.html)

2. **Fatal Pinch**: 6 months of runway + expecting investors to save you = the most common cause of death.
   - "There may be nothing founders are so prone to delude themselves about as how interested investors will be in giving them additional funding." — [The Fatal Pinch](http://www.paulgraham.com/pinch.html)

3. **The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups**:
   - Single founder / bad location / niche market / derivative idea
   - Stubbornness / hiring bad programmers / wrong platform
   - Slowness in launching / launching too early / no specific target user
   - Raising too little / too much money / spending too much
   - Poor investor management / founder conflicts / half-hearted effort / giving up
   - Source: [The 18 Mistakes](http://www.paulgraham.com/startupmistakes.html)

4. **How Not to Die**: The official cause of death is running out of money or a key founder leaving. The real cause is **demoralization**.
   - "Bad shit is coming. It always is in a startup. So don't get demoralized." — [How Not to Die](http://www.paulgraham.com/die.html)
   - "If you can just not quit, you will eventually succeed."

### Crisis Self-Diagnosis
- How many months of runway do you have at current burn rate?
- If revenue keeps growing at its current trend, do you reach profitability within runway?
- What's your concrete Plan B (surviving without investment)?

## §4. Fundraising — "The best way to convince investors is to actually be doing well."

### Core Principles

1. **Best Way to Convince**: Evidence that you're actually growing.
   - "The best way to convince investors is to make a startup that's actually doing well, and then simply tell investors so." — [How to Convince Investors](http://www.paulgraham.com/convince.html)

2. **Fundraising Dynamics**:
   - Investors are herd animals — once one commits, others follow
   - The core of negotiating leverage is not needing the money
   - If you're ramen profitable, you don't need investment, so you get better terms
   - Source: [How to Raise Money](http://www.paulgraham.com/fr.html)

3. **The Equity Equation**: If you give away X% of your company, the company's value must increase by at least 1/(1-X).
   - Source: [The Equity Equation](http://www.paulgraham.com/equity.html)

4. **Playing House Warning**: Don't get seduced by fancy offices, hiring, and formal processes.
   - "While imitating all the outward forms of a startup they have neglected the one thing that's actually essential: making something people want." — [Before the Startup](http://www.paulgraham.com/before.html)

5. **Fundraising Survival**:
   - Finish as fast as possible — fundraising distracts from real work
   - Don't take rejection personally
   - Talk to multiple investors in parallel
   - Source: [A Fundraising Survival Guide](http://www.paulgraham.com/fundraising.html)

## §5. Willpower & Motivation — "If you just don't quit, you'll eventually succeed."

### Core Principles

1. **Determination > Intelligence**: The most important quality in startups.
   - "We learned quickly that the most important predictor of success is determination." — [The Anatomy of Determination](http://www.paulgraham.com/determination.html)

2. **Relentlessly Resourceful**: The best two-word description of a good founder.
   - "Not merely relentless... In any interesting domain, the difficulties will be novel." — [Relentlessly Resourceful](http://www.paulgraham.com/relres.html)

3. **The Right Kind of Stubborn**: Persistence and obstinacy are different things.
   - Bad stubbornness: insisting reality is wrong, refusing to change direction
   - Good persistence: attached to the goal, flexible on the method
   - "Persistence is adherence to a particular goal. Obstinacy is adherence to a particular approach." — [The Right Kind of Stubborn](http://www.paulgraham.com/persistence.html)

4. **Earnestness**: The most underrated founder quality.
   - "Earnestness is the opposite of gaming the system." — [Earnestness](http://www.paulgraham.com/earnest.html)

5. **Morale is Everything**:
   - "Startups run on morale." — [How Not to Die](http://www.paulgraham.com/die.html)
   - Even a handful of ecstatic users is enough to sustain you.

## §6. Competition — "Focus on users, not competitors."

### Core Principles

1. **Late = Good Sign**: If you have a good idea, you'll feel like you're too late. That's a positive signal.
   - "Worrying that you're late is one of the signs of a good idea." — [How to Get Startup Ideas](http://www.paulgraham.com/startupideas.html)

2. **Competitors Rarely Kill**: Startups almost never die from competition.
   - "Whether you succeed depends far more on you than on your competitors."

3. **Crowded Market = Demand**: A crowded market is evidence that demand exists and no existing solution is good enough.

4. **Secret Weapon**: You need a specific thesis about what incumbents are overlooking.
   - "You have to be able to phrase it in terms of something the incumbents are overlooking."

5. **Think Differently**: Independent thinking is essential.
   - "You don't want to start a startup to do something that everyone agrees is a good idea, or there will already be other companies doing it." — [How to Think for Yourself](http://www.paulgraham.com/think.html)

## §7. Product Decisions — "Make something people want."

### Core Principles

1. **Make Something People Want**: YC's motto and the answer to almost everything.

2. **Talk to Users**: Talk to users directly. Not dashboards — conversations.
   - "What have I learned from users? The first thing: most startups have the same problems." — [What I've Learned from Users](http://www.paulgraham.com/users.html)
   - Experience your users' problems firsthand (E la Carte founder worked as a waiter)

3. **Iterate Fast**: Launch quickly and iterate quickly.
   - "Launching teaches you what you should have been building." — [Startups in 13 Sentences](http://www.paulgraham.com/13sentences.html)

4. **Toys are Good**: Projects dismissed as "toys" are often the best ideas.
   - Apple, Google, Facebook were all initially dismissed as toys.

5. **Founder Mode**: Don't take your hands off the product even as you scale.
   - Brian Chesky / Steve Jobs: deeply involved in details
   - "The conventional wisdom about how to run larger companies is mistaken." — [Founder Mode](http://www.paulgraham.com/foundermode.html)

## §8. Scaling — "Do things that don't scale → then figure out how to scale."

### Core Principles

1. **Manual First**: Automation comes later. Recruit and support users manually first.
   - Source: [Do Things that Don't Scale](http://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html)

2. **Growth Rate as Compass**: Measure weekly growth rate and let it drive your decisions.
   - Source: [Startup = Growth](http://www.paulgraham.com/growth.html)

3. **Founder Mode at Scale**: Even as you grow, the CEO should stay involved in product details.
   - Source: [Founder Mode](http://www.paulgraham.com/foundermode.html)

## §9. Life Philosophy — "Life is short. Do what matters."

### Core Principles

1. **Life is Short**: 52 weekends per year. 8 Christmases where your kid believes in magic (ages 3-10). Time is discrete.
   - "If you find yourself thinking that life is too short for something, you should try to eliminate it." — [Life is Short](http://www.paulgraham.com/vb.html)

2. **How to Do What You Love**: Blur the line between work and play.
   - "The test of whether people love what they do is whether they'd do it even if they weren't paid for it." — [How to Do What You Love](http://www.paulgraham.com/love.html)
   - Beware: don't let prestige lure you into the wrong work.

3. **A Project of One's Own**: People with their own projects are the happiest.
   - Source: [A Project of One's Own](http://www.paulgraham.com/own.html)

4. **Bus Ticket Theory of Genius**: Genius = ability + obsessive interest in a specific topic.
   - "The bus ticket collector's love is disinterested." — [The Bus Ticket Theory of Genius](http://www.paulgraham.com/genius.html)

## §10. People — "Trust your gut about people."

### Core Principles

1. **Trust Gut on People**: Question your business instincts, but follow your instincts about people.
   - "If someone seems slippery, or bogus, or a jerk, don't ignore it." — [Before the Startup](http://www.paulgraham.com/before.html)

2. **What We Look for in Founders**: Determination, flexibility, imagination, naughtiness, friendship.
   - Source: [What We Look for in Founders](http://www.paulgraham.com/founders.html)

3. **Mean People Fail**: Mean people tend to fail in startups.
   - "In the startup world, you win by creating wealth, not by capturing it." — [Mean People Fail](http://www.paulgraham.com/mean.html)

4. **Cofounders are Critical**: Work with people you've known a long time and genuinely like.

## §11. Execution — "It's normal for early work to be unimpressive."

### Core Principles

1. **Early Work is Ugly**: Even great projects look unimpressive at first. Push through it.
   - "One of the biggest things holding people back from doing great work is the fear of making something lame." — [Early Work](http://www.paulgraham.com/early.html)

2. **The Lesson to Unlearn**: Drop the "gaming the system" mindset you learned in school.
   - "Startups are as impersonal as physics." — [The Lesson to Unlearn](http://www.paulgraham.com/lesson.html)

3. **Schlep Through It**: Embrace the tedious work. It becomes your moat.
   - Stripe embraced the schlep of payment processing and had almost no competition because of it.
   - Source: [Schlep Blindness](http://www.paulgraham.com/schlep.html)

4. **Ship at 80%**: Don't wait for perfection. Launch and learn.
   - "Launching teaches you what you should have been building."

## §12. Productivity — "The top idea in your mind determines your life."

### Core Principles

1. **Top Idea in Your Mind**: Whatever you think about in the shower is your real top priority.
   - Warning: if fundraising or a dispute becomes your top idea, your product suffers.
   - Source: [The Top Idea in Your Mind](http://www.paulgraham.com/top.html)

2. **Maker's Schedule vs Manager's Schedule**: 
   - Makers need half-day blocks of uninterrupted time.
   - "A single meeting can blow a whole afternoon." — [Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule](http://www.paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html)

3. **Good Procrastination**: Putting off less important work to do more important work is good.
   - Three types: (a) doing nothing, (b) doing something less important, (c) doing something more important → (c) is good procrastination
   - Source: [Good and Bad Procrastination](http://www.paulgraham.com/procrastination.html)

4. **How to Work Hard**: If you find interesting work, working hard becomes natural.
   - "The most important thing is to work on the right thing." — [How to Work Hard](http://www.paulgraham.com/hwh.html)

## §BONUS. Airbnb Case Study

Every principle applied to one company — PG's most-cited example.

- **Organic idea**: Couldn't make rent, so they rented out air mattresses — [The Airbnbs](http://www.paulgraham.com/airbnbs.html)
- **Well, not broad**: Convention attendees only → all travelers
- **Do things that don't scale**: Personally visited NYC hosts, took professional photos — [Do Things that Don't Scale](http://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html)
- **Ramen profitable**: Reaching ramen profitability was the goal during YC — [Ramen Profitable](http://www.paulgraham.com/ramenprofitable.html)
- **Never give up**: An investor walked out of a café meeting and never came back. Binder full of maxed-out credit cards.
- **Earnestness**: 10 years in, Brian Chesky was still taking a page of notes about new ideas at dinner — [The Airbnbs](http://www.paulgraham.com/airbnbs.html)
- **PG's email to Fred Wilson**: "There's no reason this couldn't be as big as eBay." — [Subject: Airbnb](http://www.paulgraham.com/airbnb.html)
