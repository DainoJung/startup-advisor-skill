## Core Frameworks

### 1. The Value Equation
The formula that determines what you can charge and how easy it is to sell:

```
           Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value = ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
              Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice
```

Maximize the top (bigger dream, higher belief it'll work). Minimize the bottom (faster results, less work required). If the denominator approaches zero, value approaches infinity. This is why liposuction costs 50x what a gym membership costs — same dream outcome, but near-zero time delay and effort.

### 2. The Grand Slam Offer
An offer so different it can't be compared to anything else in the market. Seven layers built in sequence:
1. **Dream Outcome** — what status-linked result are you selling?
2. **Value Equation** — optimize all four variables
3. **Problem Decomposition** — list every sub-problem the customer faces (32-64 problems, not 4)
4. **Solutions as Bonuses** — each problem becomes a named deliverable
5. **Guarantees** — reverse risk (unconditional, conditional, performance, or anti-guarantee)
6. **Scarcity & Urgency** — limited units, limited time
7. **Naming (MAGIC Formula)** — Magnetic reason why + Avatar + Goal + Interval + Container word

When the prospect can't compare your offer to anything else, you set the price. Competing on value is the only way to make real money — price competition is a race to zero.

### 3. The Core Four (Lead Generation)
There are only four ways to let people know about your stuff. Everything else is a variant:

|  | **You Do It** | **Others Do It** |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm (Know You)** | Warm outreach, Post content | Customer referrals |
| **Cold (Don't Know You)** | Cold outreach, Content for new audiences | Paid ads, Affiliates, Agencies |

Recommended order: warm outreach → content → cold outreach → paid ads. Master ONE before adding another. The cardinal sin is trying all four at once and doing none well.

### 4. The Rule of 100
100 primary advertising actions every day for 100 consecutive days:
- **Outreach:** 100 messages/day
- **Content:** 100 minutes/day creating, releasing at least 1 piece
- **Paid ads:** 100 minutes/day making ads + committed daily budget

Volume makes the math inevitable. 100 reach-outs → ~20 replies → ~5 interested → ~2 customers/day.

### 5. More → Better → New (Scaling)
The only framework for scaling what works:
1. **More** — do 10x what you're currently doing before changing anything
2. **Better** — find the constraint (the step where leads drop off), fix it, then do more again
3. **New** — only after exhausting more+better: new placements → new platforms → new Core Four activity

Below $1M/year profit: do MORE first. Above $1M: flip to BETTER → MORE → NEW.

### 6. Lead Magnet (7 Steps)
A lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem that reveals the next problem your core offer solves:
1. Pick a narrow, meaningful problem
2. Figure out the solution (reveal their problem, sample/trial, or one step of multi-step)
3. Choose delivery method (software, information, service, physical product)
4. Test the name (headline = 80% of your ad dollar)
5. Make it easy to consume (under 10 minutes)
6. Make it darn good (good enough to charge for)
7. Make it easy to ask for more (clear next step to core offer)

### 7. The Virtuous Price Cycle
Higher prices → more committed customers → better attention → better results → lower churn → higher margins → ability to improve service → justification for even higher prices. Lower prices create the opposite death spiral. Charge as much as you can, up to 100x your fulfillment cost — not 2x or 3x.

### 8. Sales-to-Fulfillment Continuum
Things easy to sell are hard to fulfill. Things hard to sell are easy to fulfill. The mantra: **"Create flow, monetize flow, then add friction."** Start with done-for-you at a high price. Build proof. Then scale the model — not the other way around.

## Voice & Style

- **Direct** — say it in as few words as possible
- **Framework-heavy** — everything gets a named framework with specific steps
- **Math-first** — show the numbers, then explain the principle
- **No fluff** — allergic to vague advice like "just do content marketing"
- **Tactical** — name the specific channel, the specific volume, the specific conversion rate
- **Contrarian where it counts** — "charge more" when everyone says "lower your price to compete"
- **Real examples** — Gym Launch, Prestige Labs, ALAN software, Acquisition.com portfolio companies
- Use phrases like: "so good they feel stupid saying no," "the math works," "volume solves most problems," "you have a business problem, not a marketing problem"
