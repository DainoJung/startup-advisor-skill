## §1. Offers & Pricing — "Make people an offer so good they feel stupid saying no."

### The Value Equation

The formula that governs what you can charge and how easy it is to sell:

```
           Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value = ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
              Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice
```

**Variable 1: Dream Outcome**
The aspirational end-state the customer wants. Not your product — their fantasy. All markets ultimately sell status proxies: B2B = money (which is status), B2C = appearance, health, relationships (which are status). The more directly your dream outcome connects to status, the more people will pay.

> "The dream outcome is what separates whether someone's even interested in your category of offer or not." — Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*

**Variable 2: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement**
How confident the customer is they'll actually get the result. This is why a surgeon's 10,000th patient pays more than the first — people pay for certainty, not just skill.

> "If we can get somebody more convicted that what we're going to do is going to work, then they're actually, in a very real way, going to receive more value." — Alex Hormozi, [Capitalism.com Q&A](https://www.capitalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Alex-Hormozi-QA-100M-Offers-Building-Product-Brands-You-Can-Sell-1.pdf)

**Variable 3: Time Delay**
The gap between buying and benefiting. Lives in the denominator — it divides your value. Fast beats free, always.

> "Personal training — you got to arm wrestle somebody for an hour to get them to buy a $2,000 package because they might get their six-pack 12 to 18 months later. Whereas with liposuction, you're going to go to sleep and wake up significantly thinner. The time delay is so much shorter, and because of that it increases the value overall." — Alex Hormozi, ["How To Craft A $100M Offer In 6 Minutes"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nJl_wbt0Xqs)

The perfect end-state: "If someone were able to click a button on a website and immediately look at their stomach and have a six-pack, that would be incredibly valuable."

**Variable 4: Effort & Sacrifice**
Effort = things you have to START doing that you don't want to. Sacrifice = things you have to STOP doing that you want to keep doing (Taco Tuesday, sleeping in). This is why done-for-you beats do-it-yourself — and why it commands 10-100x the price.

> "Effort are the things that you have to begin doing that you don't want to do as a result of a purchase. Sacrifices are the things you have to stop doing that you want to keep doing." — Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*

The London Underground example: they raised rider satisfaction more cheaply with a dotted wait-time display than with faster trains. A psychological fix (reducing perceived effort/uncertainty) beat a logical one.

### The Grand Slam Offer — 7 Layers

A deliberately engineered package where every element amplifies every other element:

**Layer 1: Dream Outcome** — Choose your market, identify the dream. Key insight: sell the destination, not the plane flight.

**Layer 2: Value Equation** — Optimize all four variables. The gap between a $5 product and a $50,000 product in the same category is entirely explained by the Value Equation.

**Layer 3: Problem Decomposition** — Hormozi's distinctive contribution. List every single thing a customer has to DO, STOP doing, fears, and obstacles. Not 4 problems — 32 to 64.

> "How can I solve every single problem that your prospect has? And if you don't know what they are, that's why we do this really in-depth chapter." — Alex Hormozi, ["How To Create A GRAND SLAM Offer"](https://www.youtube.com/@AlexHormozi)

Weight loss example:
- "I don't know what to eat" → Meal plans with grocery lists
- "I don't have time to cook" → 15-minute recipes or meal delivery partnership
- "I'll be hungry" → Approved snack list
- "I'll lose motivation" → Accountability partner matching
- "My family won't eat this food" → Family-friendly recipe variations
- "I've failed before" → Restart protocol with no shame

Each solution becomes a potential bonus or core deliverable.

**Layer 4: Bonuses** — Solutions from Layer 3 become named bonuses deployed strategically:
- Reserve bonuses (2-3): held back, deployed to handle specific objections during the sale
- Close sweeteners (1-2): mentioned at the close to tip undecided prospects
- Post-purchase surprises: delivered after purchase as surprise-and-delight

> "The salesman doesn't need to say all seven of the things that you have. The idea is that you make the ask on the initial offer. If they say no, you figure out the constraint, and then you plug that bonus in." — Alex Hormozi, ["How To Craft A $100M Offer In 6 Minutes"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nJl_wbt0Xqs)

Critical rule: bonuses replace discounts. Instead of lowering price when someone objects, ADD value.

**Layer 5: Guarantees** — Risk is the single biggest objection. Changing only the guarantee can lift conversion 2-4x. Four types:
1. **Unconditional** — Money back, no questions asked
2. **Conditional** — "Do X, Y, Z — if you don't get the result, money back"
3. **Performance/Implied** — "We'll work with you until you hit [result]" or results-based pricing
4. **Anti-Guarantee** — "All sales final. If you need a guarantee, this isn't for you." (for exclusive or easily copied work)

**Layer 6: Scarcity & Urgency** — Enhancers that change buying conditions, not the offer itself.
- Scarcity = limited units (total cap, growth rate cap, cohort cap)
- Urgency = limited time (cohort-based, seasonal, pricing-based, exploding opportunity)
- The Chanel model: every bag is one-of-a-kind, stores never know what they'll get
- Last 3% of a launch timeline produces 50-60% of sales

> "You always have to remember — when demand increases, you cut supply." — Alex Hormozi, ["How To Charge Exorbitant Prices"](https://www.youtube.com/@AlexHormozi)

> "Always, always, always clear the pipe. Announce it. Always own your price increases." — Alex Hormozi, ["How I RAISE PRICES"](https://www.youtube.com/@AlexHormozi)

**Layer 7: Naming (MAGIC Formula)** — Make your offer name so the right person recognizes it as theirs:
- **M**agnetic reason why
- **A**vatar (target audience)
- **G**oal (desired outcome)
- **I**nterval (timeframe)
- **C**ontainer word (challenge, system, blueprint, etc.)

3-5 of the 5 elements is typical. Shorter beats complete. If you can rhyme or alliterate, do it — but don't force it.

### Pricing Principles

1. **The Virtuous Price Cycle**: Higher prices → more committed customers → better attention → better results → lower churn → higher margins → ability to improve service → justification for even higher prices. The opposite cycle (low price → uncommitted → bad results → high churn) kills businesses.

2. **Charge up to 100x fulfillment cost** — not 2x or 3x. A portfolio photography client went from $300 average ticket to $1,500 and weekly profit from $1,000 to $38,000.

3. **Price anchoring**: To get to $3,000 from $2,000, talk about a $4,000 price point first. Then introduce a prepayment discount that lands at the target.

4. **Never discount the main offer**: Instead of lowering prices, increase perceived value by adding bonuses. A wine study showed the same beverage tasted significantly better when participants believed it was more expensive.

> "If a business cannot pay $100-200 to get someone in the door, they don't have a marketing problem, they have a business problem." — Alex Hormozi, ["4 Steps to Maximizing Revenue Per Customer"](https://www.youtube.com/@AlexHormozi)

### Sales-to-Fulfillment Continuum

Easy to sell = hard to fulfill. Hard to sell = easy to fulfill. The mantra:

> "Create flow, monetize flow, then add friction." — Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*

Gym Launch origin story: Hormozi flew to gyms for 21 days, did everything himself for a refundable $500 deposit, selling ~$100,000/month alone. Only later did he teach owners to do it themselves at a third of the price.

Start with done-for-you at a high price. Build proof. Then scale the model.

### The Success Hierarchy

**Market > Offer > Persuasion**

Most entrepreneurs spend years improving closing skills while ignoring they're in a terrible market. Four indicators of a good market:
1. **Massive pain** — they desperately want a solution
2. **Purchasing power** — they can actually pay
3. **Easy to target** — you can reach them
4. **Growing** — tailwind behind you

Health, wealth, and relationships are the three markets that always exist.

### Self-Diagnosis Questions
- Can your prospect compare your offer to anything else on the market? (If yes, your offer isn't differentiated enough)
- Which variable of the Value Equation is weakest in your current offer?
- How many of your prospect's problems does your offer explicitly solve? (If <10, you haven't done enough problem decomposition)
- What's your fulfillment cost relative to your price? (If less than 10x, you're undercharging)
- Do you have a guarantee? What type?

## §2. Lead Generation — "There are only four ways to let people know about your stuff."

### The Core Four

Every advertising method that has ever existed fits into a 2×2 matrix:

| | **1-to-1 (Private)** | **1-to-Many (Public)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm (Know You)** | Warm outreach | Post free content |
| **Cold (Don't Know You)** | Cold outreach | Run paid ads |

> "You can talk to people one-on-one, you can make content, you can run ads, or you can get affiliates and referrals." — Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*

No fifth method exists. If you're not getting enough leads, you're not doing one or more of these with enough skill or volume.

### Channel 1: Warm Outreach

Reaching out to people who already know you — phone contacts, past customers, LinkedIn connections, followers.

**The ACA Framework:**
1. **Acknowledge** — use their name + something specific and current about their situation
2. **Compliment** — one genuine, specific observation (not generic flattery)
3. **Ask** — "Who do you know who [fits description]?" NOT "Do you want to [buy my thing]?"

The phrasing matters enormously. "Who do you know" removes pressure — people who fit will volunteer themselves, and people who don't can refer without awkwardness.

**Math**: 100 reach-outs/day → ~20 replies → ~5 interested → ~2 customers/day → 14/week → 60/month. At $400/product = $104,000/year. Zero media spend.

### Channel 2: Content

Critical distinction most businesses miss:
- **Warm content** (posting for followers) = lead NURTURE, not generation
- **Cold content** (reaching new audiences) = lead GENERATION

In phases 1-2, content supports outreach. In phases 3-5, content becomes its own lead generation engine.

Every piece follows **Hook → Retain → Reward**:
- Hook: stops the scroll (first 3 seconds)
- Retain: keeps them watching/reading (delivers value)
- Reward: makes them glad they stayed (CTA: follow, comment, DM)

> "Give away your secrets, sell the implementation." — Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*

The 3.5:1 ratio: Television runs 47 minutes content per 13 minutes ads — and that's the MINIMUM for a mature platform. Hormozi's strategy goes further: give in public, ask in private. Let the audience self-select when they're ready to buy.

**Document, don't create.** Share what you're doing, not what you think people want to hear. Volume > quality at first. One platform, go deep — don't spread thin.

### Channel 3: Cold Outreach

Private messages to strangers — cold email, cold DM, cold calling. Built on a targeted list.

**The process:**
1. Build a list of 1,000 people matching your ideal customer profile
2. Personalize the hook — first line references something specific about them
3. Lead with value — free audit, insight, resource
4. Make the ask small — "Can I send you a 3-minute video?" not "Book a call"
5. Follow up relentlessly — 80% of deals happen after the 5th follow-up; most people stop at 2

**Math**: 100 messages/day → 1-5% response rate → 1-5 conversations/day → 30-150/month → close 10% → 3-15 customers. That's a business.

The list matters more than the message. A generic list of "coaches" converts far worse than a narrow list matched to one specific situation.

### Channel 4: Paid Ads

The fastest path to scale, but ONLY if your economics work. Prerequisites:
- A proven offer (organic must convert first — ads amplify what already works)
- LTGP:CAC ratio ≥ 3:1

**Framework:**
- Test aggressively: 10 different creatives, 5 audiences, 3 offers. Kill losers fast, scale winners.
- Track to revenue, not leads. A $10 lead that never buys is worthless. A $100 lead that spends $10,000 is gold.
- Retarget relentlessly — 95% won't buy the first time. Retarget for 90 days.
- Top advertisers test 11x more creative than average.

> "If a business cannot pay $100-200 to get someone in the door, they don't have a marketing problem, they have a business problem." — Alex Hormozi

### The Recommended Progression

1. **Start with warm outreach** (free, highest conversion, proves the offer)
2. **Add one content platform** (supports outreach, builds long-term compounding asset)
3. **Add cold outreach** (scales beyond your personal network)
4. **Add referral system** (leverages happy customers)
5. **Add paid ads** (only after offer is proven and unit economics work)
6. **Add affiliates/agencies** (only after you can manage external partners)

### Self-Diagnosis Questions
- How many of the Core Four are you actually running? At what volume?
- Are you doing 100 primary actions per day? If not, your volume problem comes before any strategy problem.
- Which channel are you trying to master? (If the answer is "all of them," that's your problem.)
- Do you have an owned audience (email list, followers)? If not, content isn't lead generating — it's going into a void.

## §3. The Rule of 100 & Volume — "Most advertising fails because the volume is a fraction of what's required."

### The Rule of 100

100 primary advertising actions every day for 100 consecutive days:

| Channel | Daily Action |
|---|---|
| Warm outreach | 100 personal messages |
| Cold outreach | 100 outreach messages |
| Content | 100 minutes creating, releasing ≥1 piece |
| Paid ads | 100 minutes making ads + committed daily budget |

> Hormozi distributed 300 flyers and got zero leads. His mentor tested with 5,000 — and ran 5,000 per day for a month. Most entrepreneurs aren't doing half of what's required; they're doing a tiny fraction.

Volume makes the math inevitable. You can't learn without reps. You can't scale mediocre ads profitably. The only way to find exceptional ads is to make a ton of them.

### The Volume Story

Gym Launch benchmarks with warm outreach:
- 100 reach-outs → ~20 replies → ~5 interested → ~2 paying
- 500 weekly reach-outs at $400/product = $104,000/year
- That's twice the U.S. median household income, at zero media spend

### Self-Diagnosis Questions
- How many outreach messages did you send yesterday? (If < 100, do more before changing anything)
- How many pieces of content did you publish this week?
- How long have you been running your current channel consistently? (If < 100 days, don't switch)

## §4. Lead Magnets — "A complete solution to a narrow problem."

### What a Lead Magnet Is

A free or low-cost offer that:
- Solves a specific, painful problem RIGHT NOW
- Requires minimal effort to consume (under 10 minutes)
- Naturally leads to your paid offer by revealing the NEXT problem

### The 7-Step Framework

**Step 1: Pick the Problem**
Narrow and meaningful. Must lead to your core offer. "Every problem has a solution. Every solution reveals more problems." Pick a problem where the revealed next problem is what your core offer solves.

**Step 2: Figure Out the Solution** (one of three types)

| Type | How It Works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Reveal their problem | Diagnose a problem they didn't know they had | Website speed test, posture analysis |
| Sample/Trial | Limited access to your core offer | Free month, 3 free uses, demo |
| One step of multi-step | Solve step 1 of a 5-step process | Free template, calculator, checklist |

**Step 3: Choose Delivery Method**
- **Software** — spreadsheet, calculator, small tool
- **Information** — course, lesson, interview, tips
- **Service** — do work for free
- **Physical product** — something they can hold

4 delivery methods × 3 types = 12 possible lead magnets. Make as many versions as you can and rotate them.

**Step 4: Test the Name**

> "When you have written your headline, you have spent 80 cents of your dollar." — David Ogilvy (cited by Hormozi)

Improving the headline can 2x, 3x, or 10x engagement. Test headline → images → subheadline, in that order.

**Step 5: Make It Easy to Consume**
Under 10 minutes ideally. Nobody reads a 200-page PDF lead magnet.

**Step 6: Make It Darn Good**
Good enough to charge for. Your free stuff builds or destroys your reputation — 99% of people won't buy, but they WILL tell others.

**Step 7: Make It Easy to Ask for More**
Clear next step to your core offer. Example: podcast episode on getting leads → "I have 11 more tips on my website" → lead magnet form → thank you page displays paid offer.

### Bad Lead Magnets
- "Join our newsletter" (no specific value)
- 200-page PDF (too much work)
- Generic checklists (not specific enough to a painful problem)

### Self-Diagnosis Questions
- Does your lead magnet solve a complete, narrow problem? Or is it a teaser that frustrates?
- Can someone consume it in under 10 minutes?
- Does it naturally reveal the next problem your core offer solves?
- Is it good enough you could charge for it?

## §5. Scaling — "More → Better → New"

### The Framework

The only three ways to scale any channel:

**1. MORE** — Do 10x what you're currently doing. Even with no improvements, doubling inputs gets more output.
- Below $1M/year profit: do MORE first. You haven't done enough volume for percentage changes to matter.

**2. BETTER** — Find the constraint (the step where most leads drop off), fix it, do more again.
- Above $1M/year profit: flip to BETTER → MORE → NEW. Making things better can be lowest cost, highest return.
- One test per week per platform. Log results. Scale winners.

**3. NEW** — Only after exhausting more+better. In this order:
1. New placements on the same platform (Instagram: stories → messenger → reels)
2. Same placements on a new platform (Instagram reels → YouTube Shorts)
3. New placements on new platform
4. Entirely new Core Four activity

> "What will get me the most leads for the least amount of work? That is the rule." — Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*

The "Size of the Pie Fallacy": businesses mistakenly think they've saturated their market once they see a little success. There are more leads out there than you can possibly imagine.

### The 3Ds of Team Scaling

To scale past personal capacity, replace yourself as the primary lead getter:
1. **Document** — turn your process into a step-by-step checklist
2. **Demonstrate** — perform the task in front of the employee using the checklist
3. **Duplicate** — have the employee perform the task in front of you until they replicate results

Leverage math: trade 40 hours of doing for 4 hours of managing. Repeat across multiple employees.

### Lead Getters — Four Types

People who advertise your stuff for you:

| Type | Description | When to Add |
|---|---|---|
| **Customers** (referrals) | Happy customers tell friends. Ask right after they experience a win, not before. | After first 10-20 happy customers |
| **Employees** | Hires who run outreach/content/ads. | After a channel is proven and needs more hands |
| **Agencies** | Outside specialists (usually paid ads). Set 6-month deadline, absorb skills, bring in-house. | When you want to buy back time |
| **Affiliates** | Other businesses promote for commission. Highest compounding leverage. | After core offer converts reliably |

Prestige Labs example: grew through 4,000+ gym affiliates — zero paid ads, zero sales team. ALAN software scaled to $1.7M/month within six months using three tiers of affiliates.

### Whisper → Tease → Shout (Launch Framework)

For activating affiliates or audiences:
- **Whisper** (months out): hint at the product, build curiosity, behind-the-scenes
- **Tease** (~30 days out): reveal the product, share value elements, set public launch date
- **Shout** (final 3 days): heavy CTAs with scarcity, urgency, and bonuses

Last 3% of time = 50-60% of sales.

### Self-Diagnosis Questions
- Have you maxed out volume on your current channel before trying to optimize? (If not, do MORE)
- What's the single step where the most leads drop off? (That's your constraint — fix it)
- Are you trying a new channel because the current one is genuinely exhausted, or because it got hard? (Usually the latter)
- Do you have documented, repeatable processes for your lead generation? (If not, you can't hire)

## §6. Goodwill & Retention — "Improve the product until customers become your sales force."

### The Goodwill Engine

Referrals come from goodwill. Goodwill comes from the gap between what customers expected and what they got. To maximize it:

1. **Sell better customers** — narrow targeting to people who get the most value
2. **Set better expectations** — slowly lower promises until close rates dip, then stop. Now you've maximized the surprise gap.
3. **Get more people better results** — find what your best customers do differently, force all customers to do the same:
   1. Survey customers for best results
   2. Interview them to find common actions
   3. Force new customers to repeat those actions
   4. Match guarantee conditions to those actions

### Content Strategy for Trust

Give in public, ask in private:
- Television's 3.5:1 give-to-ask ratio is the MINIMUM
- Integrated asks: 1.5 minutes of promotion in a 60-minute podcast
- Intermittent asks: 1 promotional post for every 10 value posts
- People become uncomfortable receiving without reciprocating — they'll seek you out

> "Give away your secrets, sell the implementation. Your free stuff builds or destroys your reputation." — Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads*

### Self-Diagnosis Questions
- When was the last time a customer referred someone without being asked?
- What do your best customers do that your average customers don't?
- Are you asking for referrals right after a win, or randomly?

## §8. Key Formulas & Math

### LTGP:CAC Ratio
- **LTGP** = Lifetime Gross Profit per customer
- **CAC** = Customer Acquisition Cost
- **Target: LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1** (observed threshold for scaling)
- Improve by: reducing CAC (better ads/targeting/lead magnets) OR increasing LTGP (better model, higher margins, more purchases/upsells)

### The Only 3 Ways to Grow
1. Get more customers
2. Increase their average purchase value
3. Get them to buy more times

### Warm Outreach Math
- 100 reach-outs/day × 20% reply rate = 20 conversations
- 20 conversations × 25% genuine interest = 5 qualified leads
- 5 qualified × 40% close rate = 2 customers/day
- 2 × 5 days × $400 = $4,000/week = $208,000/year

### Cold Outreach Math
- 100 messages/day × 1-5% response = 1-5 conversations
- 30 days × 3 avg conversations = 90 conversations
- 90 × 10% close = 9 customers/month
- Cost of outreach effort should stay under 1/3 of customer's lifetime profit

### Paid Ads Math
- $50 CPL × 10% close rate = $500 cost per customer
- If LTV is $2,000, profit = $1,500 per customer
- That's a money printer — scale it
